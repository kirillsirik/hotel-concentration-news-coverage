# Hotel Concentration and Local News Coverage of Short-Term Rentals

Kirill Sirik, Princeton University. November 2026.

## Abstract

Consolidation can strengthen incumbents' incentives to seek restrictions on entrants. I study whether the 2016 hotel acquisitions, chiefly Marriott's purchase of Starwood, changed local newspaper coverage of short-term rentals. Among 326,251 news articles from 613 U.S. cities, a language model identifies 283 about short-term rentals, and this coverage rises sharply when a city first regulates them. Coverage did not grow more where the acquisitions raised hotel concentration more, which gives no indication that consolidation led hotels to make their case against short-term rentals through local newspapers. With controls for pre-merger concentration, size, news volume, and Airbnb presence, the 95 percent confidence intervals rule out increases in coverage of short-term rentals, and in coverage opposing them, larger than about a tenth of their post-merger means for each 100 points that the acquisitions added to a city's HHI. The bound is looser where the acquisitions added 100 points or more. Across cities, coverage appears to rise with exposure once pre-merger concentration is held fixed, but when the larger pre-merger Airbnb presence of exposed cities is also held fixed, the rise disappears.
